The Claim Contract

What a claim actually is once /ref:extract commits it — every field, why it's there, and how the same claim survives (or doesn't) a re-extraction.

Primary source: docs/concepts.html, “The claim contract” — including the real worked example below, taken from that page verbatim (a scratch-library claim, placeholder PMID). Field validation itself: validate_claim in skills/ref-manager/scripts/lib_schema.py. This lesson assumes Lesson 0003 — it picks up exactly where that one's Stage 4 (/ref:extract) left off.

A claim is one reported finding

Not one paper, not one paragraph — one finding. A single paper with three reported results produces three claim objects, each a separate entry in that version's claims.json array. The ref-extractor subagent (Lesson 0003) proposes the content; extract.py fills in defaults, validates every claim against validate_claim, assigns IDs, and commits.

Every field, and why it exists

Three groups, not one flat bag of fields:

1. The 12 normalized fields — what was found

You already met these in Lesson 0003: population, intervention, comparator, outcome, timepoint, direction, effect_value, effect_measure, uncertainty_interval, study_design, cohort_identity, adjustment_context. Every key must be present; the literal string "unknown" counts as present.

2. Provenance fields — where it came from

locator (section/page/table/figure), evidence_span (a direct quote, not a paraphrase), evidence_tier (abstract or full), source_hash, and paper-level study_type (the study's overall design, distinct from this claim's own study_design — a paper can be mixed overall while one claim's study_design says specifically RCT sub-study).

3. Identity & lifecycle fields — how it's tracked over time

FieldWhat it means
claim_idopaque c- + 12 hex, assigned by extract.py
content_hashsha256 of the 12 normalized fields + study_type — the claim's fingerprint
statusactive, or superseded once a rerun replaces it
excluded_from_synthesisbool, becomes true after a human reject (/ref:verify)
supersedes / superseded_byoptional links between old and new claim at the same locator
status and excluded_from_synthesis are independent axes. A claim can be active and excluded (a human rejected it, but nothing has superseded it yet), or superseded and never excluded (a routine re-extraction replaced it, no human judgment involved). Retrieval filters on both: status='active' AND excluded_from_synthesis=0. Neither field alone tells you whether a claim is actually in play.

Stable claim IDs across reruns

claim_registry.json stores every claim ever committed for a paper — nothing is ever deleted from it. When you re-run /ref:extract on a paper, assign_claim_ids compares each incoming claim against whatever was active at that same locator before the run:

  1. Same content_hash: the claim keeps its existing claim_id. Nothing changed, so nothing new is minted.
  2. No hash match, exactly one unconsumed prior claim at that locator: a genuine revision. The new claim gets a fresh ID and supersedes pointing at the old one; the old claim flips to status: superseded with superseded_by set — and stays in the registry, not deleted.
  3. No hash match, zero or several prior claims at that locator: ambiguous — no supersession is asserted, the new claim just gets a fresh ID.

This is the same immutable-history instinct from Lesson 0001's raw/<sha256>/ directories, one layer up: nothing about a claim's history is ever overwritten, only superseded and kept.

You re-run /ref:extract on a paper. At locator "Table 2", the extractor now reports a different effect_value than before, and there was exactly one prior active claim at that locator. What happens to the old claim?

Right — a content_hash mismatch with exactly one unconsumed prior claim at the locator is the textbook supersession case. The old claim is kept, marked superseded, never edited or deleted.

Not quite — the registry never deletes or edits a prior claim in place. A genuine revision at the same locator supersedes it: new claim_id, old one kept with status: superseded and superseded_by set.

You run /ref:verify claim <pmid> <claim_id> reject. Which field changes on that claim?

Right — "rejected" isn't a status value at all. Rejection sets excluded_from_synthesis, a separate axis from status (active/superseded), and retrieval checks both.

Not quite — status only ever tracks active/superseded (a supersession event, not a human judgment). A reject correction sets excluded_from_synthesis instead, leaving status alone.

Want to see a claim actually flip to superseded, or watch excluded_from_synthesis change after a real /ref:verify reject? Ask your agent to run it against a scratch library and show you the before/after JSON.
