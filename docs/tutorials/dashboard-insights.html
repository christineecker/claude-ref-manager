Tutorial

Explore the evidence in the dashboard

The dashboard's Insights tab turns extracted claims, the concept graph and appraisal batches into interactive views: what is notable, how a paper or concept connects, where recorded relations disagree, which population × outcome cells have no evidence, which topics are growing, and how mature the evidence for each intervention is. Everything is read-only and deterministic; every action it suggests is a command you copy and run.

What the views read

The tab is computed in your browser from one payload, /api/knowledge in serve mode or the same data embedded in a --static build, so both modes show the same views. It only has what earlier commands committed, and each view says which command fills it when it is empty:

DataComes fromFeeds
Active claims (population, intervention, comparator, outcome, timepoint, direction, extraction tier, design)/ref:add (abstract tier), /ref:extract (full tier)evidence map, gaps, claim network, neighbourhood, maturity, synthesis
Concepts and their aliases/ref:concept, /ref:weaveneighbourhood, gap grid, maturity; claim values and MeSH terms that match a name or alias count as that concept
Relations with review state and rationale/ref:weave (propose, create, review)claim network, neighbourhood, open-conflict counts
MeSH terms, authors, note excerpts/ref:add, /ref:notetopic timeline, concepts graph, papers & authors, clusters
Appraisal batches (GRADE, RoB 2 / Newcastle-Ottawa / AMSTAR 2)/ref:review --batchmaturity's appraisal overlay, paper panels in the graph

Scope. With follow the Papers filters ticked (the default), the tab covers only the papers the Papers tab currently shows: search, project, issue and source filters. Add a year range next to it to narrow further. The line above the views counts the papers and active claims in scope.

Steps

  1. Start from the notable cards

    The row of cards at the top is computed only from the loaded data and changes with the scope. Each card is a shortcut:

    CardCountsClick to
    links on the best-connected paperRegistry concepts the paper mentions plus other papers that share one of its authors. Claims don't count, so heavily extracted papers don't win on claim count alone.Open that paper's neighbourhood
    open conflictsUnreviewed potential_conflict relations plus contradicts relations that went stale, touching papers in scopeFilter the Papers tab to those papers
    single-study findingsIntervention → outcome pairs backed by one paperFilter the Papers tab to those papers
    papers with no active claimsPapers in scope that /ref:extract hasn't reachedFilter the Papers tab to them
    growing topic / fading topicThe cluster with the largest / smallest trend ratio (step 6)Open Clusters filtered to that topic

  2. Centre the graph on one paper or concept

    Open Knowledge graph → Neighborhood and choose a concept or paper in Center on…. You can also press Center here on any selected graph node, or Show in graph in a paper's side panel. The centre sits in the middle; its direct links form the first ring, and 2 hops adds a second ring of what those link to.

    Links in a neighbourhood are: a paper mentions a concept (MeSH term, title or claim field matching a name or alias), an author authored a paper, a paper reports a claim, a claim is about a concept, a claim is a supporting claim of a relation, and recorded relations between concepts. Concepts are always registry concepts, never guessed from free text.

    Click a node, or press Enter or Space on it, to select it. The side panel lists its papers, findings, relations and connections, with Open paper and Center here. For a paper that belongs to a /ref:review batch, it also shows the batch's GRADE certainty and the paper's risk-of-bias result. While a node is selected, the arrow keys step through its neighbours.

    A paper with no concept, author or claim links shows an empty state with the fix to copy: /ref:extract <pmid> for claims, then /ref:weave to map them to concepts.

  3. Read the claim network

    Claim network draws interventions, outcomes and populations from the claims in scope. An intervention → outcome line is coloured by the direction most claims report. Recorded relations are overlaid, each type with its own line and a glyph badge, so the difference is readable without colour:

    Under the graph, the legend doubles as a filter. Click a relation type to hide or show it, tick reviewed only to hide unreviewed relations, and set Extraction to full text only to ignore abstract-tier claims. Hidden relations also stop pulling nodes into a neighbourhood. The side panel lists Recorded relations, with open ones first, and Mixed direction pairs. Mixed direction means the claims disagree; it is not a reviewed judgement.

  4. Review a potential conflict

    Click a relation's line or badge, or its entry in any list, to open its detail panel:

    • Type and review state, the reviewed date, the rationale (or "none recorded"), and a stale warning if a supporting claim changed.
    • Supporting claims, one block per claim, with direction, population, comparator, timepoint and effect measure. Any field that differs between the claims, or is missing from one, is marked ≠. These are the fields that decide whether two opposite findings are really comparable.
    • The papers behind the relation, and a Papers button that filters the Papers tab to them.
    • For unreviewed or stale relations, the review command to copy.

    illustrative output

    › /ref:weave review rel-de5834755e5b contradicts --rationale "Same population, comparator and 12-week timepoint; opposite directions in two adequately powered samples"
{ "relation_id": "rel-de5834755e5b", "type": "contradicts", "review_state": "reviewed", … }

    Press the dashboard's refresh button (↻). It re-fetches /api/knowledge, bypassing the server's cache, and the dashed ? edge becomes a solid ✕ with your rationale in the panel. If none of the ≠ fields hold up on reading the papers, review the relation as another type instead. The graph discovery tutorial explains review in depth.

  5. Find the uncovered population × outcome cells

    Gaps opens on the Population × outcome grid. Choose an intervention concept (each option shows its claims and gaps). Rows are the populations and columns the outcomes of that intervention's own claims. A filled cell shows how many papers cover it, coloured by effect direction; a hatched cell marked gap has no claim.

    This is exactly the rule /ref:gaps … --intervention-concept applies, so the two always agree:

    • A claim belongs to the intervention when its intervention matches the concept's name or an alias (ignoring case).
    • A population or outcome that matches any concept's name or alias counts as that concept. Once "SBP" is an alias of systolic blood pressure, both land in one column. Other values stay as written, and nothing is fuzzy-matched.
    • Placeholder values ("unknown", "not reported", "n/a", …) are not a population or outcome.

    Click a gap cell to see the claims that establish its population and its outcome (population_evidenced_by, outcome_evidenced_by). The panel also offers Copy PubMed query, Copy /ref:search-pubmed, Candidate papers, and the matching /ref:gaps command.

    illustrative output

    › /ref:gaps --project tdcs-review --intervention-concept tdcs --types population_outcome_gap
{ "gaps": { "population_outcome_gap": [ { "missing_population": "older adults", "missing_outcome": "working memory", "population_evidenced_by": [ … ], "outcome_evidenced_by": [ … ] } ] } }
    Sparse pairs (heuristic) is the other Gaps view. It ranks any two claim fields that rarely appear together by papers on each side ÷ (1 + papers together). It is a prompt for ideas, not a /ref:gaps finding, and is labelled that way. Both views also list single-study findings and unresolved conflicts; a conflict's name opens it in the claim network.
    A gap means this library has no claim for that combination. It is not evidence that the literature lacks one; search before concluding anything.

  6. See which topics are growing

    Clusters groups topic terms (MeSH headings, concepts, claim populations, interventions and outcomes) that appear in at least two papers. Two terms are linked only when they share papers at least 1.5× as often as chance would give. Terms that appear in most papers co-occur with everything, so plain counts would merge every topic into one cluster. Scopes under 20 papers use plain counts.

    Each card shows the cluster's terms, papers, year span, a papers-per-year sparkline (last five years shaded) and a trend:

    TrendRule
    ↑ growingpapers in the last five years ≥ 1.25 × papers in the five before
    ↓ fading≤ 0.8 ×
    → stablein between
    no labeleither window has fewer than 3 papers; the counts are still shown

    The windows end at the newest publication year in the library, not today's date, so a static snapshot reads the same next year. Each card shows both counts and the ratio.

    Map draws clusters as circles, sized by papers and coloured like their cards, joined when two clusters share at least 2 papers. Click a cluster to see its trend, the clusters it overlaps with, and its terms. Table lists the same clusters and overlaps as text.

  7. Judge how mature the evidence is

    Maturity gives one row per intervention concept with claims in scope. The five measures sit side by side, each with its counts and a bar, and are never blended into one score:

    MeasureDefinition
    Paperspapers with an active claim whose intervention is this concept (name or alias)
    Outcome breadthits distinct outcomes ÷ outcomes studied for any intervention in scope
    Coveragepopulation × outcome cells with a claim ÷ all cells (step 5's grid)
    Full-text shareits claims extracted from full text ÷ all its claims
    Open conflictsunreviewed potential conflicts plus stale contradictions on relations touching the concept; click the number to open the first one

    The Appraisal column and the panel below it show your /ref:review batches that include papers in scope. For each batch you see:

    • its GRADE certainty (high, moderate, low, very low), baseline and number of downgrades
    • the five GRADE factors, each marked downgraded, no downgrade or not assessed, with the reason
    • each paper's checklist (RoB 2, Newcastle-Ottawa, AMSTAR 2) with its result: high risk, assessed, no high-risk domain, insufficient information or no checklist, and whether any domain is human-reviewed
    GRADE is per batch, not per outcome. /ref:review records one certainty for a batch's whole set of papers, and the dashboard shows it that way. Reviews recorded after a batch was frozen (with verify.py review-appraisal) are included without a --refresh. With no batches, the panel offers /ref:review --project <slug> --batch <label> to copy; see the systematic review tutorial.

    Each row links to that concept's Gap grid and Neighborhood.

  8. Rearrange the graph, or read it as a table

    The knowledge graph (all four modes) and the cluster map are interactive:

    DoResult
    Drag a nodeIt moves and its lines follow. It stays where you drop it until you press Re-layout or the data changes. A press that barely moves still counts as a click.
    Drag the backgroundPans the view (with a mouse or pen; on touch screens the page scrolls as usual)
    ⌘ / Ctrl + scroll, or pinchZooms around the pointer; plain scrolling still scrolls the page. The − / + / ⤢ buttons zoom and reset too.
    Shift + arrow keysMove the focused node without a mouse
    Re-layoutDiscards dragged positions and lays the graph out again
    TableLists every node (type, papers, links, and hop in a neighbourhood; sortable) and every edge (kind and papers). Relation entries open their detail panel.

    In global modes, Nodes ≤ (20–200) caps how many nodes are drawn. Lower it on large libraries, raise it for detail. Neighbourhoods use their fixed 100-node cap instead.

Freshness and large libraries

  • Serve mode caches the knowledge payload and rebuilds it when claims, notes, authorship, appraisal corrections, concepts, relations or review batches change on disk. The refresh button (↻) always rebuilds it.
  • Static mode is a snapshot: rebuild with /ref:dashboard --static after running /ref:extract, /ref:weave or /ref:review.
  • Views are recomputed when the scope changes, not while you hover or drag. On a 1,200-paper library most views redraw in under 20 ms, and a scope change that rebuilds the graph index takes well under 100 ms.

Putting it together
